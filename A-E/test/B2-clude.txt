That evening, the commander of the Nadew Command gave a briefing on the battle situation to some senior commanders, political directorate and political section heads, and political commissars who had come from Asmara. Although all of them already knew about the course of the battle, they had to listen without tiring. For me, however, it was new. I was also fortunate to be there. The overall battle plan was as follows. The Second Liberation Revolutionary Army would be deployed on three main fronts, namely the Afabet, Kerkbet, and Algena fronts. The command deployed on the Kerkbet front, comprising the 21st and 24th divisions along with an armored brigade, was called the Mebreq Command. The one deployed on the Algena front, comprising the 19th, 15th, and 23rd divisions, was called the Wuqaw Command. The primary mission of the Mebreq and Wuqaw commands was to conduct a defensive battle to prevent the enemy from breaking out of Nakfa or breaking in to reinforce its strength, while attacking from west to east and from north to south. The mission of breaking through and entering Nakfa — which had long served as the enemy's rear base and headquarters — was assigned to the Nadew Command, organized around the 5th, 12th, and 22nd mountain divisions. The offensive operation was planned to begin on February 8, 1974 at 2:00 in the morning. Accordingly, the 3rd, 17th, and 22nd divisions had begun moving northward from Afabet. The movement had been underway for three days. I arrived just as the last force of the 22nd division was moving out. Most of them were young people from the lowlands. And none of them seemed to be heading to battle zones. They were chatting and laughing. I can't remember their faces. But everyone's face seemed like mine. It was my face... it was the face of Ethiopia.

All of them had their missions. The 3rd division, departing from Afabet and continuing its march toward Mamado, was to take the Mabela valley, block the road connecting Nakfa and Algena, and then attack the enemy force concentrated on this front that was obstructing the revolutionary army's movement, preventing them from supporting each other. The mission given to the 17th division was to depart from the Mamid headquarters, follow the Kemchewa stream, pass through Tiksin and the Harewa mountain at elevation point 2059, and after taking offensive action from east to west at elevation points 1728, 1755, and 1702, capture Nakfa.
